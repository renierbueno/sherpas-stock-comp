# 05 · Business lens (internal)

Internal working hypotheses used to make decisions in this case. Not a section of the deliverable. Every line is an assumption until a source or Sherpas confirms it.

## Customers

- Large advisory firms are the target user [brief]. A capability that helps win or keep large firms should weigh more than one only small independent advisors value. [ASSUMPTION: confirm]
- Advisors who serve tech or corporate employees meet stock compensation often; those who serve retirees or small-business owners meet it less. [ASSUMPTION: confirm with research]

## Value to Sherpas

- Stock compensation is a gap today ("nowhere to put it") [brief]. A household with significant equity pay gets a score that misses part of its wealth, its concentration risk and its future cash flows, which can weaken the advisor's trust in the analysis. [ASSUMPTION: confirm]
- Equity pay events (vests, expirations, trading windows) create recurring moments for the advisor to contact the client. [ASSUMPTION: confirm]
- Equity pay leads naturally into tax planning, one of the areas Sherpas says it is investing in [web, see `01_context.md`]. [ASSUMPTION: confirm]

## Pricing and monetisation

- Unknown how Sherpas charges (per seat, per firm, per analysis). Recorded as an open question; no pricing proposal without it. [ASSUMPTION: confirm]
- Default stance: stock compensation as part of the core analysis, not a paid add-on, because a gap in the core diagnosis is a quality problem, not an upsell. To be revisited in F3. [ASSUMPTION: confirm]

## What would change these

- Data on how many of Sherpas' clients' households hold employer equity.
- How Sherpas prices, and whether large firms buy by module.
